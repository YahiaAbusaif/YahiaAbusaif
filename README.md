# Yahia Abusaif

Hey. I love problem solving. Ad hoc problems, the competitive-programming kind, and the work ones too, where I sit with a teammate on the technical side, or sit with the business and talk about what is actually wrong. I loved data analysis as a student, and the job is where it got real. I like looking into the data until the business can actually see the product. At Sahl the fun one was pulling too many analytics tools into one picture of the app, deep links and campaigns included. At Microsoft, campaign priority was less fun, and still fun. I still like being the person who reads the data and then says what we should do.

I want that written down, mainly so someone who joins later, or misses the meeting, can catch up. I also like challenging the design. Not arguing for the sake of it. Either we move it to a better state, or we find out why this version is the right one for our case, and the people who only reviewed it learn something too. Pairing is not how I like to do that. Two people on a call can agree and forget to write the reason, and then everyone else is missing a piece. I mentor on the light side. More than that turns into micromanagement, and people stop learning. I also like to be left alone for a while: draft it, know it, then talk about the outcome.

On the computer science side, I am curious about data science, cybersecurity, and how open source projects are adapting now. I have not gone deep on all of that yet.
